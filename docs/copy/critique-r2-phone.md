# Phone reader — round 2

Same thumb as round 1: QR during the five minutes, thirty seconds, no
context, the speaker still talking. I read the revised phone layers cold, in
the order the page gives them (nameplate → start line → Play/dial → stage →
"Send this to myself" → `teaser.text`), then checked each of my round-1
objections against the writer's `## answers`. Unresolved ones are re-raised
below with new numbers; everything not listed is resolved.

Severity as in round 1: **lost** / **deaf** / **aside**.

---

## draft: claim

Round 1: 17 objections. Resolved: 16. Re-raised: 1 (P-c-2 → P-c-18).

### front

**P-c-18** (deaf; re-raises P-c-2) — blurb possibles: *"Existing and mattering
are different questions; here you answer them one at a time."* "Verbs" became
"questions" and I still do not know what exists or matters, and "here you
answer them" is not true on the phone I am holding (I cannot write). It is
the one blurb of the four that gives my ear nothing. The other three now do:
"the crossing can be made inaudible", "cannot hold kick, hat, kick, snare",
"the ear hears the difference". Fix (13 words, the toy's own second half):
*"A machine walks a tree of motifs someone wrote. Only the machine speaks."*
— or keep the first clause and let the second be the sound (17 words):
*"A machine walks a tree someone wrote, and only the machine speaks. Existing
and mattering come apart."*

Which would I tap first? Still statistical-feedback, but motif-memory's
"can be made inaudible" is now a close second. The `.sub` and `what-is` are
unchanged and fine.

### motif-memory

Cold read: nameplate is the claim and gives me nothing to do, but "each
remembers what it just played" tells me what kind of thing I am about to
hear. Start line *"press ▶ Play: five voices invent on one pulse."* — I press.
Running line *"press memory: at the next bar each voice loops. Turn fidelity
down and listen for a seam."* — I press memory, wait a bar, hear the loop,
turn the dial. This works in thirty seconds. The writer's choice (no dial on
the nameplate, gesture in the running line) costs me nothing here because
the start line is a plain instruction and the running line arrives the
moment I obey it.

**P-c-19** (deaf, mild) — `teaser.running`: *"listen for a seam."* A seam in
what? The nameplate said "crossing", the running line says "seam", and
`teaser.text` says neither; three lines, two names, no definition my ear can
use. "seam" is honest (the claim is that I will not find one) but a thumb
reads it as a typo for "sound" at 11.5 px. The question draft's *"still a
loop?"* asks my ear the same thing in words it already has from the previous
clause. Fix (83 chars, keeps the bar-line guard): *"press memory: next bar,
each voice loops. Turn fidelity down and listen for a seam."* only if the
author wants "seam"; otherwise (75): *"press memory: next bar, each voice
loops. Turn fidelity down: still a loop?"*

`teaser.text`: *"Press memory: at the next bar line every voice loops what it
just played, up to two bars. Fidelity is how much of that it keeps."* — the
question draft's gloss has been borrowed and it is the right sentence. Good.
`teaser.shut` *"it is running but muted or at zero; only the desktop page can
change that."* — one glance. Good. `share` — unchanged; I would tap.

### markov

Cold read: nameplate *"One step of memory buys a style, never kick, hat,
kick, snare. Two chains, one dial: style to dice."* — I know there is a dial
and what its ends are. Start line *"turn structure: at 1 a written style, at
0 dice."* — I turn. Both running lines name a direction and a thing to hear.
Usable; the tightest markov phone of the three.

**P-c-20** (aside, mild) — `teaser.running.low`: *"what is written returns."*
I did not write anything, so "what is written" is a thing I have to infer
someone else did. The draft's own `teaser.text` has the phrase that says it:
"what a hand wrote". Fix (80): *"turn structure up: what a hand wrote
returns; kick, hat, kick, snare never will."*

### statistical-feedback

Cold read: nameplate *"Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference.
Six voices and a silence keep books on one pulse."* — the claim, no dial, and
"keep books" is a picture I can hold. Start line: press Play. Running line
*"turn heterogeneity up: the same shares, now in clumps and droughts, like
dice."* — the most hearable cue on any sf page: I know exactly what dice
sound like now. Usable.

**P-c-21** (aside, a day later) — `share`: *"Dice have no memory, and the ear
hears the difference. The ledgers themselves want a desktop."* On a friend's
phone "the ledgers themselves" has no antecedent: nothing before it said
ledger, voice or book. Round 1's "Six voices, one ledger" at least told me
what the ledgers were. Fix (15 words): *"Dice have no memory, and the ear
hears the difference. Seven ledgers want a desktop."* — or (18) *"Dice have
no memory; the ear hears the difference. Six voices keep books: the ledgers
want a desktop."*

### possibles

Cold read: nameplate *"Existing and mattering are different questions, and a
hand answers them one at a time. Only the machine speaks."* — nothing to do
and nothing to hear until the last four words; "a hand answers them" is not
my hand. Start line: press Play. Running line *"turn bias and listen: left,
the figure sharpens; right, it dissolves, then inverts."* — I turn left, I
hear the figure tighten; right, it loosens. Usable, but the nameplate spent
its twenty words on the half of the claim the phone cannot test, and the
running line rescues it.

**P-c-22** (deaf, mild; the same word is in all three drafts) —
`teaser.running` and `teaser.text`: *"then inverts."* The academic's
correction is right and the word is not a sound. Turning the dial to the far
right, what I hear is not "an inversion"; it is the figure I was hearing
being replaced by one I was not. If the spec allows "the light branches win"
at the far right, say it in ear words (87): *"turn bias: left, the figure
sharpens; right, it dissolves, then the rare branches lead."* If it does not,
keep "inverts" and accept that the last word of the line is for the desk.

`teaser.text` *"You cannot strike a drum here: bias only leans how it
chooses."* — P-c-17 fixed properly. `share` *"A hand wrote a motif tree"* —
fixed.

## draft: question

Round 1: 13 objections. Resolved: 13. Re-raised: 0.

### front

All four blurbs now read once; *"A motif exists. Does it matter yet?"* is a
question I understand even if I cannot yet answer it. `.sub` has "audio"
back. I would tap motif-memory first, as in round 1. No objection.

### motif-memory

Cold read: nameplate *"Five voices keep their last two bars; *fidelity* is
how much each replays. Can you hear one start to remember?"* — the dial on
my screen is named and glossed before I touch it, so when I get to it I know
what it is. Start line: press Play. Running line *"press memory: next bar,
each voice loops what it played. Ride fidelity down: still a loop?"* — bar
line, loop, dial, ear, 90 characters. This is the best motif-memory running
line of the three, and the only one that keeps the bar-line guard AND a cue.
Usable in well under thirty seconds. No objection.

The risk I named in round 1 (turning fidelity before memory) is closed by
the start line being a plain "press ▶ Play" and the running line putting
memory first; the glossed nameplate does not send my thumb to the dial
early, because the dial is below the start line and I read in order.

### markov

Cold read: nameplate *"Two chains, one step of memory apiece, and a dial from
grammar to dice. Can one step hold a phrase?"* — dial named. Start line
*"turn structure: 1 is the grammar as written, 0 is dice."* — I turn. Running
high *"turn structure down: whatever grammar is written goes to dice. Was
there a phrase in it?"* — "whatever grammar is written" is a mouthful but it
tells me what happens, then asks. Usable.

**P-q-14** (aside) — `teaser.running.low`: *"turn structure up: the weights
are read. Does kick, hat, kick, snare ever come round?"* "the weights" are a
desktop noun the phone never shows, and "are read" tells my ear nothing
about what I will hear when I turn up. The question that follows is the good
part. Fix (90): *"turn structure up: what a hand wrote returns. Does kick,
hat, kick, snare ever come round?"*

### statistical-feedback

Cold read: nameplate *"Six voices and a silence keep books; a dial loosens the
reading. Can the ear tell dice from a ledger?"* — dial named, question asked.
Start line: press Play. Running line *"turn heterogeneity up: each plays as
often, less predictably. Does it sound like dice yet?"* — I know what holds
(how often), what changes (how predictably), and what to listen for. Usable.

The two-questions split (nameplate asks "dice from a ledger", `teaser.text`
asks "the same shares sound like dice") stands by the writer's refusal
(C-q-14) and the directions. From my chair it no longer bites: the nameplate
now carries the dial, so the top question has a gesture; I do not reach the
bottom paragraph in thirty seconds; and the one who does has by then heard
what the dial does. I withdraw P-q-9 rather than re-raise it.

### possibles

Cold read: nameplate *"A hand writes what can follow what; a machine walks it
aloud. A motif exists. Does it matter yet?"* — four short sentences; "A hand
writes" is not my hand, and I notice it, but it does not send me looking for
a write button. Start line *"press ▶ Play: the machine walks a tree of motifs
aloud."* Running line *"turn bias left: the figure sharpens. Right: it
loosens, then inverts. Which motifs appear?"* — direction, cue, and "which
motifs appear?" is something I can actually listen for when I turn right.
Usable.

**P-q-15** (deaf, mild) — *"then inverts"*: the same word as P-c-22; the same
remark. If "the rare branches lead" is allowed (84): *"turn bias left: the
figure sharpens. Right: it loosens, then the rare branches lead."* — and the
question moves to `teaser.text`, which already has it ("which motifs did you
not know were there?").

## verdict

Toy by toy, which draft's phone layer I can use in thirty seconds now:

- **motif-memory — question.** Its running line is the one that fits the
  bar-line guard, the loop and an ear cue into 90 characters (*"press memory:
  next bar, each voice loops what it played. Ride fidelity down: still a
  loop?"*), and its nameplate glosses the dial before I reach it. The
  rulesheet's "the loop wanders" is the better cue and should be borrowed if
  the bar-line guard goes back in (P-r-13); the claim's "seam" is the weakest
  (P-c-19). All three are usable; round 1's winner (rulesheet) slipped only
  by dropping the guard.
- **markov — claim.** Nameplate, start line and both running lines are each
  one gesture and one thing to hear, nothing to infer; the rulesheet is a
  close second ("hear the walk dissolve into dice"); the question draft's
  "the weights are read" (P-q-14) is the one phone line in the round that
  names a desktop noun and gives the ear nothing.
- **statistical-feedback — question**, by a hair over the claim. The
  question's nameplate names my dial and its running line tells me what
  holds, what changes and what to listen for; the claim's "clumps and
  droughts, like dice" is the most hearable cue of the three and the author
  should consider putting it in whichever draft wins. All three are usable;
  in round 1 two of them sent me looking for a memory switch, and none does
  now.
- **possibles — rulesheet.** Nameplate tells me who wrote, who speaks and
  that I will not; running line is clean on the left and middle. The claim's
  nameplate spends its words on the half of the claim the phone cannot test
  (P-c-18's blurb has the same problem); the question's is usable.

Round-1 objections resolved: **claim 16 of 17**, **question 13 of 13**,
**rulesheet 11 of 12** (one refusal I accept). New this round: claim 5
(P-c-18 … P-c-22, one a re-raise), question 2 (P-q-14, P-q-15), rulesheet 4
(P-r-13 … P-r-16, one a re-raise).

The one thing still wrong for a phone, and it is small: **the corrections
that came in for honesty's sake arrived as desk words.** "inverts", "turns
contrary", "what is written", "the weights are read", "seam" are each true
and each is the last thing on a line whose job was to tell my ear what to
expect. Every draft's phone layer is now usable on every toy — nothing sends
my thumb looking for a control it does not have, and every running line
names a gesture and a cue — so the remaining work is one word per line, not
a rule. The round-1 structural fault (nameplate written for the desk; ear
cue below the share button) is gone from all three.
