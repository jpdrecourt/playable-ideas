## motif-memory
### shows
Memory is a state of a voice. Throw one's MEM switch, ride its *fidelity* down, and listen for the crossing.
### meta.description
Five random voices share one pulse. A switch makes a voice remember its last two bars, and a knob rides it back to invention without changing how much it plays.
### og.description
Memory as a state of a voice, not a recorder beside the band. Make five random voices remember what they just played, then ride them back to invention.
### text
**Memory is a state of a voice,** not a recorder beside the ensemble. Every voice keeps its last two bars, unasked. Throw MEM and those bars become its score at the next bar line. You decide a passage was worth keeping, never which window: nothing to aim, nothing to hit on the beat.

**Fidelity is the crossing.** The knob decides how much of the stream is score and how much invention. Leave density alone and the amount of music never moves; only where it falls does. A crossing that changed both at once would announce itself; this one only does if you make it.

**The paper's §5** finds nouns but no verbs. This is one verb, remember, on each voice.
### teaser.text
**Memory is a state of a voice.** Press memory and every voice keeps the two bars it just played. Then ride fidelity down and try to hear where memory ends and invention begins: the amount of music never moves, only where it falls.

## markov
### shows
One step of memory buys a style and never kick, hat, kick, snare. Two chains, thirty-two weights: tap a cell.
### meta.description
Two Markov chains, four states each, thirty-two weights, and each remembers one step. Build a matrix and hear what one step of memory holds and what it cannot.
### og.description
One step of memory buys a style and never kick, hat, kick, snare. Two first-order chains, thirty-two weights, and a dial from grammar to dice.
### text
**One step of memory buys a style.** Each state owns one row of odds. Lean kick toward hat, hat toward snare, snare back toward kick, and every step answers the one before it: no two bars are the same bar.

**And never kick, hat, kick, snare.** The kick must lead to the hat the first time and to the snare the second, and its row is one set of odds: nowhere to put the difference. Give it both and the chain picks at random. The model's shape, not a shortage of weights.

**Two chains share nothing.** Sixteen weights each, none across them: the paper's §3.2, counted. In the Conductor a hand plays the matrix — here it is alone.
### teaser.text
**One step of memory buys a style.** At 1 the chains read the example as written and every step answers the last. Turn structure down and the grammar dissolves into dice. Nowhere on the dial is a phrase: kick, hat, kick, snare needs two steps and each chain has one.

## statistical-feedback
### shows
Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference. Six voices share one pulse by keeping books: turn memory off and hear the same weights as dice.
### meta.description
Six voices and a silence share one pulse by keeping books: each turn goes to whoever is furthest behind. Turn the ledger off and the same weights become dice.
### og.description
Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference. Ames' fair-share selection as a toy: six voices, a ledger of attention, and one switch that turns it into dice.
### text
**Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference.** Six voices and a silence each keep a ledger, the bar beside the name: turns taken, less turns owed. The next turn goes to whoever is furthest behind, and the seven bars always sum to zero. That is Charles Ames' fair share, and it is the whole law.

**Turn memory off** and selection stops reading the books, though they go on being kept: the same weights become dice, the stream clumps and droughts, and the starved voices sink further left. Turn it back on and they are paid at once — a burst of exactly the voices that were owed. That payoff is the argument in one gesture.
### teaser.text
**Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference.** Six voices and a silence share one pulse, and each turn goes to whoever is furthest behind its fair share. Turn heterogeneity up and the shares lose their grain toward dice: proportions never move, only how predictably each comes round.

## possibles
### shows
Existing and mattering are different questions: write makes a branch exist, heavier makes it matter, and only the machine speaks.
### meta.description
You write a tree of what can follow what, five symbols and a weight on every branch, and a machine walks it aloud. Nothing you do makes a sound.
### og.description
Existing and mattering are different questions, and an instrument can let you answer them one at a time. A motif tree written by hand, and a machine that alone speaks.
### text
**Existing and mattering are different questions.** Press write on a strip — kick, say — and a branch exists under the finger; write snare next and the motif kick, snare exists. That is one verb. heavier and lighter are the other: how much a branch matters, and at zero it is cut away.

**In Assayag and Dubnov's model the two are one act:** a performance repeats itself, and the repetition both creates a motif and raises its count. Take the performance away and they come apart.

**The machine is the only speaker.** There is no way to strike a drum; a figure you want at a particular moment is exactly what you cannot have, and that refusal is the claim.
### teaser.text
**The machine is the only speaker.** A hand wrote this tree of what follows what — kick, snare, cowbell, crash, rest — and ▶ Play sets the machine walking it. Nothing you touch makes a sound: turn bias left and the heavy branches win, right and the light ones do.

## notes
- **Character counts.** Each parenthesised number is the sentence's own length, without the keyboard clause. Add 32 to every `notStarted` on the three transport pages (" Or tab to Play and press Enter."), 42 to markov's desktop `notStarted` and 37 to its phone one. The worst case is markov's desktop empty `notStarted`: 87 + 42 = 129, inside 145. The markov `stuck` and `running.one` counts (125, 110) are measured with the placeholders as written; with the longest names the code can insert ("snare" and "cowbell"; "walker 1" and "walker 2") they are 131 and 117, both inside 145. Word counts below are `wc -w` counts, so an em-dash standing alone counts as a word.
- **`shows` budget.** statistical-feedback is the one page given the ≤ 30-word allowance (28 words): the claim is the aphorism, and the gesture that falsifies it needs "keeping books" and "turn memory off" in the same breath. The other three are at 20 / 20 / 20.
- **motif-memory gets a desktop `text`** (three blocks, 120 words) where it has none today. Reason: the desk encounter is the one the brief says may carry an argument and name the paper, and today the claim ("a state of a voice, not a structure beside the ensemble") is argued nowhere on the page — only the spec says it. The three sibling pages all have standing text, so the collection reads unevenly at the desk without it. The author may still prefer `text: false`; if so the `shows` line and `running` carry the whole gesture and nothing is lost on the phone.
- **Desktop `text` lengths:** motif-memory 120, markov 119, statistical-feedback 118, possibles 120. sf has only two blocks (the brief asks for 2–4): the claim and the one gesture; weight, stress, silence, tap-a-name and heterogeneity are left to the per-control hints, which are out of scope.
- **The phone claim on possibles is the second half of §1** ("the machine is the only speaker"), not the first ("existing and mattering"), because the phone cannot write and so cannot show the two verbs; the first half is on the front page, in `shows`, `og` and the desktop text. Say if you want the two halves matched across layers instead.
- **"amount of music never moves"** (motif-memory, desktop and phone) holds under the spec's condition that density is left where it was at capture (§3.4). The desktop text says "leave density alone"; the phone has no density control, so the unqualified form is true there. The spec's own caveat about accents at f = 0 (§3.4, "not information-theoretically clean") is not claimed against: "listen for the crossing" and "try to hear where memory ends" invite the test rather than promise its result.
- **"the ear hears the difference" on the phone** (sf): the phone's dial runs from the ledger read exactly to near-dice with the ledger still read (§8.5), never to dice themselves; the teaser text says "toward dice" for that reason, and the memory switch is named only on the desktop, where it exists.
- **"a style arrives"** (markov phone, running.low) uses the spec's own word for what one step buys (§1, "the output has a style"); "a grammar" is the spec's word for the example's matrices (teaser section). The phone `notStarted` ("1 is a grammar, 0 is dice") dropped "the example's" to stay under 53 characters.
- **Blurbs** are the four claims, as instructed. possibles' blurb rephrases the spec's claim into the paper's own "verbs" ("Whether a motif exists and how much it matters are two verbs, not one.") to stay inside 14 words; the spec's wording ("existing and mattering are different questions") is kept verbatim in `shows`, `og` and the desktop text.
- **Source names**, once each, on the desktop only: the paper's §5 (motif-memory), the paper's §3.2 (markov), Charles Ames (sf, text) and Ames (sf, og), Assayag and Dubnov (possibles). The markov text also carries the spec's own qualification ("here it is alone") so the page is not read as a verdict on the Conductor.
- **Front `what-is`** is a proposal; "none" is the alternative and loses nothing the `.sub` does not already say.
- **Unsure the mechanism supports:** "Make five random voices remember" (motif-memory og) describes the phone's one-button behaviour, which throws all five switches; on the desktop a visitor may throw one. "nothing to hit on the beat" paraphrases the spec's "nothing to press on the beat" (§1).
