# Phone reader — round 1

Who I am: I scanned the QR during the five minutes, or at the poster. One
thumb, about thirty seconds, the speaker still talking. I know the talk's title
and nothing else. I have not read the paper, the specs or the writers' notes.

What my phone shows me, top to bottom (measured against `lib/page.js`, not
assumed): the toy's name; the nameplate (`shows`, 12 px); the start line (one
`teaser.*` sentence, with the keyboard clause glued to `notStarted`); then
**▶ Play** (not on markov) and the "?" and ONE dial (`memory` + `fidelity`,
`structure`, `heterogeneity`, `bias`); then the passive stage (timeline, trail,
record, trace); then the collection's invitation and **Send this to myself**;
and only then `teaser.text`. So the standing text is the LAST thing on the
page, below the share button. In thirty seconds I read the nameplate and the
start line, I touch what is in front of me, and I read the next start line.
Whether I ever reach `teaser.text` depends on whether the first two lines got
me to scroll. I mark that where it matters.

The baseline I read first is the live copy in `toys/*/index.html`. Where a
draft keeps a baseline sentence I say so, because the objection then belongs to
all three.

Severity, in my own scale: **lost** = I did not know what to press or turn, or
I went looking for something that is not there; **deaf** = I knew what to do
but not what to listen for; **aside** = the word names a desktop thing but the
sentence tells me so, and my thumb is not misled.

## draft: claim

### front

**P-c-1** (lost) — blurb markov: *"One step of memory buys a style and never
kick, hat, kick, snare."* I read it three times. "buys a style and never
[buys] kick, hat, kick, snare" has lost its second verb, and in a list of four
one-liners I do not slow down for a riddle. Fix (12 words): *"One step of
memory: a style, yes; kick, hat, kick, snare, never."*

**P-c-2** (deaf) — blurb possibles: *"Whether a motif exists and how much it
matters are two verbs, not one."* "Two verbs" means nothing to me unless I
caught that exact minute of the talk. Nothing here says what I would hear or
touch. I would not tap it. Fix (13 words, the other half of the toy's own
claim): *"A machine walks a tree of motifs someone wrote. Only the machine
speaks."*

**P-c-3** (aside) — blurb motif-memory: *"Memory is a state of a voice, and
crossing into it can be inaudible."* "a voice" is unexplained and "crossing
into it" is abstract, but "can be inaudible" is a dare and I would tap a dare.
Keep; just know that the one word doing the work is "inaudible".

Which would I tap first? **statistical-feedback**, because *"Dice have no
memory, and the ear hears the difference"* is the only blurb that promises
something to my ear in plain words. The `.sub` tells me these go with the talk
(good: "wandering mind and clumsy hands" is the title I just heard) but says
nothing about a thumb; the `what-is` line does ("an argument you check with a
thumb"), and I would keep it for that phrase alone.

### motif-memory

**P-c-4** (lost) — `shows`: *"Throw one's MEM switch, ride its fidelity down,
and listen for the crossing."* There is no MEM switch on my phone; there is a
button that says `memory`. "one's" tells me to pick a voice and I cannot pick
one. "its fidelity" — there is one `fidelity` dial, not five. I spent my first
five seconds looking for a MEM. Fix (18 words; the desktop still reads it
correctly): *"Memory is a state of a voice. Press memory, ride *fidelity* down,
and listen for where remembering ends."*

**P-c-5** (deaf) — `shows`: *"listen for the crossing."* Crossing of what into
what? The only place that tells me ("where memory ends and invention begins")
is `teaser.text`, at the bottom of the page under the share button. The
nameplate should not use a word that the last paragraph defines.

**P-c-6** (deaf) — `teaser.running`: *"press memory and every voice keeps its
last two bars. Then ride fidelity down."* This is the baseline sentence and all
three drafts keep it in substance, so this objection is to everyone. Three
things, in the order I hit them: (a) "keeps" does not tell me I will HEAR
anything — I pressed memory and expected the sound to change; it did not,
immediately; (b) nothing says the switch lands at the next bar line, so for
up to two seconds I thought the button had failed and pressed it again (which,
per the spec, un-throws it); (c) after "ride fidelity down" there is no ear:
what am I listening for as I turn? The sentence has two gestures and no
listening cue. Fix (87 chars): *"press memory: at the next bar each voice
loops what it just played. Turn fidelity down."* "loops" is what my ear
gets; "at the next bar" stops the second press. The listening cue has to go
somewhere above the fold too — see P-c-8.

**P-c-7** (aside) — `teaser.nothing`: *"every density is at zero … the desktop
page can open them."* I do not see a density, but the sentence tells me the
desktop has them. Harmless; also I cannot reach this state from the phone's
defaults. Same for `teaser.shut` ("muted, or at zero, on the desktop page").

**P-c-8** (deaf, structural) — `teaser.text`: *"the amount of music never moves,
only where it falls."* This is the one sentence on the page that tells me
what the dial does to my ear, and it is the last sentence on the page. In
thirty seconds I never read it. Either `running` carries a five-word version
of it or the thumb goes home without the claim. (Budget is the problem: 92
chars cannot hold the two gestures AND the ear. If the author has to choose,
the ear beats "at the next bar".)

`share`: *"Memory is a state of a voice: one button and five random voices
remember what they just played."* A day later, with the title "motif-memory —
Playable Papers" above it, I would tap: "five random voices remember what they
just played" is concrete. It does not say the page wants a desktop, which is
the reason I sent it to myself; the other two drafts do. Minor.

## verdict

Toy by toy, which draft's phone layer I could actually use in thirty seconds:

- **motif-memory — rulesheet.** Its nameplate says the order (remember, then
  ride), its `nothing`/`shut` lines name nothing I cannot see, and its
  standing text is the only one that warns me about the bar line and tells me
  I will hear a loop. Borrow one sentence from the question draft: *"Fidelity
  is how much of that it keeps."* All three fail the same running line
  (P-c-6 / P-q-4 / P-r-3).
- **markov — claim.** The running pair ("dissolves into dice" / "a style
  arrives, and kick, hat, kick, snare never will") is the one I could act on
  without thinking; the question draft's `running.low` is as good, its
  `running.high` is not. Every draft's nameplate needs the cell / build / weights
  taken out (P-c-9, P-q-6, P-r-5).
- **statistical-feedback — question.** Only this draft tells my ear its job on
  the phone (*"Does it sound like dice yet?"*, *"When does it stop sounding
  like a machine?"*). All three nameplates are written for the memory switch I
  do not have (P-c-12, P-q-9, P-r-9); the claim and rulesheet versions sent
  me looking for it.
- **possibles — rulesheet.** *"turn bias. Left, the figure sharpens. Right, it
  dissolves."* is the best running line in the round, and its text names the
  trace. The question draft's running line (P-q-12) is the one place I could
  not have done anything at all.

The one thing all three drafts get wrong for a phone: **the nameplate is
written for the desk, and it is the first line I read.** On three toys out of
four, in every draft, `shows` names a control my phone does not have (MEM, a
cell, "build one", "turn memory off", write, heavier, "weight each branch")
and never names the one dial I do have. The brief's rule that a phone word
may not name an unseen control is applied to the state sentences and not to
the 12-px line above them, and the thumb does not know the difference. The
corollary is the same in all three: the sentence that tells my ear what to
listen for lives in `teaser.text`, which the page puts below the share button,
so the `running` line has to carry a listening cue of its own or the thirty
seconds end without one. The rulesheet's possibles and the claim's markov show
it can be done inside 92 characters.

Objection counts: claim 17 (P-c-1 … P-c-17), question 13 (P-q-1 … P-q-13),
rulesheet 12 (P-r-1 … P-r-12).
