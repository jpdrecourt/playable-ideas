# Critique, round 1 — the fair copy editor

Read as a working editor of English prose, against the brief's §3–§6 and §8,
the specs' §1, and the live copy. Each objection: layer, the sentence as
written, what is wrong in a line, the sentence I would put in its place (state
sentences carry their character count, keyboard clause excluded, inside the
§4 working limits of 145 desktop / 92 phone). Where a layer is good I say so
and nothing else. I do not police the talk's frame.

A convention used below: "cold" means read by someone who has not seen the
page yet (the front page, a share preview, a nameplate on a phone).

## claim

### front

**E-c-1** — `what-is`. "A playable paper is an argument you check with a thumb, and its mechanism is a spec anyone can rebuild." The second clause is a category slip: a mechanism is not a spec, a spec describes one; and "rebuild" has lost its object. → "A playable paper is an argument you check with a thumb, and a spec anyone can rebuild it from." (19 words)

**E-c-2** — `blurb markov`. "One step of memory buys a style and never kick, hat, kick, snare." The zeugma ("buys a style and never [buys] …") is the line's whole wit and, cold, its whole problem: the reader meets four drum names with no verb to hang them on and reads the line twice. The desktop can afford that; the front page cannot. → "One step of memory buys a style, and cannot hold kick, hat, kick, snare." (13 words). The same line recurs in markov `shows`, `og` and `share`; see E-c-12.

**E-c-3** — `blurb possibles`. "Whether a motif exists and how much it matters are two verbs, not one." "Whether a motif exists" is a question, not a verb, so the sentence says a question is a verb. The spec's own line is the clearer one and the "one at a time" is what makes it a toy. → "Existing and mattering are different questions; here you answer them one at a time." (14 words)

The `.sub` is the best of the three drafts' and stands.

### motif-memory

**E-c-4** — `shows`. "Throw one's MEM switch, ride its *fidelity* down, and listen for the crossing." "One's" reads first as the impersonal pronoun (one's own switch) and only on the second pass as "one voice's". → "Memory is a state of a voice: throw its MEM switch, ride *fidelity* down, listen for the crossing." (18 words). (On the phone the button says *memory*, not MEM; see across-the-drafts.)

**E-c-5** — `meta.description`. "…and a knob rides it back to invention…" In `shows` the hand rides the knob; here the knob rides the voice. Keep one picture. → "…and a knob takes it back to invention without changing how much it plays."

**E-c-6** — `og.description`. "not a recorder beside the band" — the desktop text on the same page says "beside the ensemble". Same thing, same name. "band" is the better word for a share preview and "recorder" is a good concretion of the spec's "structure"; use it in the text too, or use "ensemble" here. One or the other.

**E-c-7** — `text`, block 1. "You decide a passage was worth keeping, never which window: nothing to aim, nothing to hit on the beat." "which window" has no verb and the colon that follows makes the reader hunt for one. → "You decide that a passage was worth keeping, never which window to keep: nothing to aim, nothing to hit on the beat."

**E-c-8** — `text`, block 2. "Leave density alone and the amount of music never moves; only where it falls does." and "this one only does if you make it." Two dangling "does" in one block, each standing for a different verb (moves / announces itself). The ellipsis is the author's habit, and once is a style; twice in four lines is a tic. → "Leave density alone and the amount of music never moves; what moves is where it falls. A crossing that changed both at once would announce itself; this one announces itself only if you make it."

**E-c-9** — `text`, block 3. "This is one verb, remember, on each voice." Between commas "remember" reads as the aside ("…one verb, remember, …" = as I said). → "**The paper's §5** finds nouns but no verbs. This page puts one verb, *remember*, on every voice."

**E-c-10** — `start.notStarted`. "…and five voices start guessing on one pulse." "Guessing" is a new verb for the page; everywhere else the opposite of memory is invention. → "press ▶ Play, or the Space bar, and five voices start inventing on one pulse." (77; 109 with the clause)

**E-c-11** — `teaser.shut`, and the identical sentence on markov, statistical-feedback and possibles. "it is running and nothing reaches you: muted, or at zero, on the desktop page." The three-comma apposition is a lift from markov's live copy and was never a sentence: "at zero" has no noun, and "on the desktop page" dangles after both. On a phone at 11.5 px it is read twice or not at all. → "it is running but muted or at zero; only the desktop page can change that." (74). One sentence, four pages.

The rest of the state sentences on this page, and `teaser.text` and `share`, are good; `running` desktop and phone are the clearest on the site.

### markov

**E-c-12** — `shows`. "One step of memory buys a style and never kick, hat, kick, snare. Two chains, thirty-two weights: tap a cell." Two faults: the zeugma (E-c-2), and "tap a cell" on a nameplate the phone shows above a page with no cells. The nameplate serves both readers or it serves neither. → "One step of memory buys a style, and cannot hold kick, hat, kick, snare. Two chains, thirty-two weights, one dial." (20 words)

**E-c-13** — `meta.description`. "Two Markov chains, four states each, thirty-two weights, and each remembers one step." A clause joined by "and" to a list of nouns. → "Two Markov chains, four states each, thirty-two weights, one step of memory apiece. Build a matrix and hear what one step holds and what it cannot."

**E-c-14** — `og.description`. Same zeugma as E-c-2; take the same fix. The second sentence ("Two first-order chains, thirty-two weights, and a dial from grammar to dice") is good and should be the `shows` tail if the writer prefers it to "one dial".

**E-c-15** — `teaser.text`. "At 1 the chains read the example as written and every step answers the last. Turn structure down…" The phone reader meets "At 1" before anything has been called *structure*; and "kick, hat, kick, snare needs two steps and each chain has one" drops the noun the whole page is about. → "**One step of memory buys a style.** Structure at 1 reads the example as written, and every step answers the last. Turn it down and the grammar dissolves into dice. Nowhere on the dial is a phrase: kick, hat, kick, snare needs two steps of memory, and each chain has one."

**E-c-16** — `share`. "…The matrix wants a desktop." The page has two matrices and says so everywhere else; and the zeugma again, read cold on someone else's phone. → "One step of memory buys a style, never kick, hat, kick, snare. The matrices want a desktop." (17 words)

**E-c-17** — `start.notStarted.empty`. "Tap a cell in a rest row and the first weight starts the chain." There are two chains; the tap starts one walker (spec §3: "the first weight in a rest row starts that walker"). → "the matrices are empty. Tap a cell in a rest row: the first weight starts that walker." (86; 128 with the clause)

The desktop `text` is the best prose on this page in any draft: "no two bars are the same bar", "nowhere to put the difference", "The model's shape, not a shortage of weights." The head "**And never kick, hat, kick, snare.**" continuing block 1's head across the gap is a risk that pays. "the paper's §3.2, counted" is terse to the edge but a desk reader with the paper gets it. Keep the block. The `running.both` line ("Try to write kick, hat, kick, snare into the cells, and hear that no weights will hold it") is the right test, said plainly.

### statistical-feedback

**E-c-18** — `shows` (28 words, the one page given 30). "…Six voices share one pulse by keeping books: turn memory off and hear the same weights as dice." Three things. It names the memory switch on a nameplate the phone reader sees above a page with no memory switch. "Hear X as dice" is not an English construction the ear accepts without a pause ("hear the same weights become dice" is). And the silence, "a citizen, not a gap" in the spec and present in every other layer of this page, has been dropped. Give the gesture to `running`, where it already is, and the nameplate comes in at budget. → "Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference. Six voices and a silence share one pulse by keeping books." (20 words)

**E-c-19** — `meta.description`. "Turn the ledger off and the same weights become dice." The switch is called memory on the page and in `text` and `running`. → "Turn memory off and the same weights become dice."

**E-c-20** — `og.description`. "…a ledger of attention, and one switch that turns it into dice." "It" has three candidates (the toy, the ledger, the selection) and none becomes dice. → "…six voices, a ledger of attention, and one switch that stops reading it."

**E-c-21** — `teaser.text`. "Turn heterogeneity up and the shares lose their grain toward dice: proportions never move, only how predictably each comes round." Two metaphors pulling against each other: losing grain is smoothing, going toward dice is clumping. Then an elided verb ("only how predictably each comes round [moves]") on the phone. → "Turn heterogeneity up and the shares loosen toward dice: the proportions never move, only when each comes round."

**E-c-22** — `share`. "Six voices, one ledger: it wants a desktop." Read later on another phone, "it" has no antecedent that makes sense. The site's idiom is "the ledger itself wants a desktop" and it is better. → "Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference. Six voices; the ledger itself wants a desktop." (17 words)

**E-c-23** — `start.notStarted`. "…and one option takes every turn: six voices, or the silence." First reading: a single option takes all the turns. The colon then lists seven things as "one option". → "press ▶ Play, or the Space bar, and each turn goes to one of six voices, or to the silence." (91; 123 with the clause)

**E-c-24** — `teaser.notStarted`. "press ▶ Play and six voices and a silence take turns." Two "and"s in nine words, the first joining an instruction to a description. → "press ▶ Play: six voices and a silence take turns." (50; 82 with the clause)

**E-c-25** — `teaser.running`. "turn heterogeneity up and hear the same shares lose their grain." The live line, kept. On the desktop "grain" sits beside "regularity" and "clockwork" and pays; alone on the phone it is a word the reader has to guess at, and the guess (grainy = rough = more random) runs the wrong way. → "turn heterogeneity up and hear the same shares come round less predictably." (75)

The desktop `text` is the live copy tightened and it reads: "turns taken, less turns owed" is a better gloss of the ledger than anything in the spec, and "and it is the whole law" earns its cadence here because it closes a block that was all mechanism. Two blocks is the right number for this page.

### possibles

**E-c-26** — `shows`. "Existing and mattering are different questions: write makes a branch exist, heavier makes it matter, and only the machine speaks." Three claims in one line, two bare lowercase control names as grammatical subjects in a string that cannot bold them, and both controls absent from the phone the nameplate sits on. → "Existing and mattering are different questions: one press makes a branch exist, another makes it matter; the machine alone speaks." (20 words)

**E-c-27** — `og.description`. "…and a machine that alone speaks." Inversion for its own sake. → "A motif tree written by hand, and a machine that is the only speaker."

**E-c-28** — `text`, block 1. "That is one verb. heavier and lighter are the other: how much a branch matters, and at zero it is cut away." A sentence starting with a lowercase control name reads as a typo; the live copy bolds control names in body text and so should this (HTML is allowed here). "the other" wants its noun. → "That is one verb. **heavier** and **lighter** are the other verb: how much a branch matters, and at zero it is cut away." Bold **write** in the same block.

**E-c-29** — `teaser.text`. "A hand wrote this tree of what follows what…" Two things. "what follows what" is not "what can follow what"; the page is about the possible, and every other layer says "can". And "this tree" on a phone that shows no tree (the phone shows Play, bias and the trace) points at nothing. → "A hand wrote a tree of what can follow what — kick, snare, cowbell, crash, rest — and ▶ Play sets the machine walking it."

**E-c-30** — `start.running`. "press write on a strip and that symbol follows the finger; press heavier and the machine takes that branch more often." "Follows the finger" (the live phrase) is spatial on first reading — the symbol trails behind the finger — when the page's own `nothing` sentence has already given the right picture: "that branch exists under the finger". One picture per page. → "press write on a strip and that branch exists under the finger; press heavier and the machine takes it more often." (114)

Blocks 2 and 3 of the desktop `text`, the `share`, and the phone `running` are good.

## question

### front

**E-q-1** — `sub`. "Four browser toys, one question each, left open by a paper on an instrument with nouns but no verbs." Cold, "nouns but no verbs" is a private phrase: the visitor has not read the paper and cannot unpack it, so the sub ends in a riddle. The sub also lost "audio". The paper's fact behind the phrase is plain and concrete. → "Four browser audio toys, one question each, left open by a paper about an instrument with no memory." (18 words)

**E-q-2** — `blurb possibles`. "Does a motif that exists already matter?" "Already" attaches to "exists" on first reading (a motif that already exists) and the sentence is then a tautology; the intended reading (does it *already* matter) is the second one. The same line is `shows`, `og`, `teaser.text` head and `share` on possibles, so this is five sentences. → "A motif exists. Does it matter yet?" (7 words)

The other three blurbs and `what-is` are good: "Can the ear tell dice from a ledger?" is the best front-page line in any draft.

### motif-memory

**E-q-3** — `shows`. "…Can you hear one start to remember? Ride *fidelity*." The imperative tail has no direction and names the second gesture without the first; "Ride fidelity" alone is a label, not an instruction. → "Five voices keep what they just played. Can you hear one start to remember? Throw MEM, then ride *fidelity* down." (20 words)

**E-q-4** — `text`, block 1. "**Nobody is recording.** Every voice keeps the two bars it just played, without being asked. Throw MEM and that record becomes its score…" The head denies a recording and the second sentence names "that record"; the reader stops to reconcile them. The spec's distinction is that nobody *presses* record. "never which window" also wants "to keep" (E-c-7). → "**Nobody pressed record.** Every voice keeps the two bars it just played, without being asked. Throw MEM and those two bars become its score at the next bar line: you decided that a passage was worth keeping, never which window to keep. Is that still capture?"

**E-q-5** — `text`, block 2. "Cell by cell, on a fresh roll, it trades remembered placement for invention…" and "whether this verb is audible is for the ear." "On a fresh roll" is the spec's jargon for a fresh random draw and a desk reader hears a bread roll; "is for the ear" is missing its infinitive. → "Cell by cell it trades remembered placement for invention while the amount of music holds. The paper's §5 called remembering a verb the instrument lacked; whether this verb is audible is the ear's to say. Ride the knob down. Where did the memory go, and did you hear it leave?" The head ("**Fidelity moves where, never how much.**") and the closing question are both good.

**E-q-6** — `start.nothing`. "every density is at zero, so the pulse runs with nothing to remember. Open a gate and that voice invents again." "A gate" arrives unintroduced; the sentence has just said "density", so "one" is the pronoun. → "every density is at zero, so the pulse runs with nothing to remember. Open one and that voice invents again." (108). "Nothing to remember" is a fine touch.

**E-q-7** — `teaser.shut` (and possibles `teaser.shut`, the same sentence). "running, but muted or the master is at zero — only the desktop page can change that." The live line. "muted" (an adjective) and "the master is at zero" (a clause) are not parallel, and the phone reader does not know a master. → "it is running but muted or at zero; only the desktop page can change that." (74)

`start.notStarted` ("…and each quietly keeps what it plays") is good; "quietly" is the one adverb in three drafts that is doing work. `running` desktop and phone are good.

### markov

**E-q-8** — `meta.description`. "A first-order Markov chain: two walkers, four states each…" One chain or two? The page, the spec and every other layer say two chains. → "Two first-order Markov chains: four states each, thirty-two weights, one step of memory. Build a style, then ask it for a phrase. A playable paper."

**E-q-9** — `og.description`. "Two chains, four states, thirty-two weights." Four states *each*; without it the arithmetic is wrong on a line that is all arithmetic. → "Two chains, four states each, thirty-two weights."

**E-q-10** — `text`, block 1. "The paper's §3.2 counted what more would cost; this page asks what one step buys." "What more" is too vague for the sentence's whole point. → "The paper's §3.2 counted what a second step would cost; this page asks what one step buys."

**E-q-11** — `text`, block 3. "Listen for whether the figure ever comes round." and "not a state, not a weight, not a die." "Listen for whether" is a clause stuffed into a prepositional phrase; in this style the question is the natural form. "A die" is correct and nobody says it; the spec's "random stream" is both accurate and sayable. → "Which cell holds that difference? Does the figure ever come round? The second walker shares nothing with the first: not a state, not a weight, not a random stream. A richer chain, or just another one?"

**E-q-12** — `teaser.text`. "At 1 the dial reads the written grammar; at 0 every next state is a quarter chance." A dial does not read; the walk reads, and "the dial" has not yet been called *structure*. → "At 1, structure reads the grammar as written; at 0 every next state is a quarter chance. Turn it. Does kick, hat, kick, snare ever come round?" ("each remembers only the state it stands on", just before, is the best one-line gloss of first order in the round.)

**E-q-13** — `start.notStarted.empty`. "…the first weight starts the audio and the chain at once." The live line. "At once" means both *immediately* and *together*, and the sentence needs the second. → "tap a cell in a rest row: the first weight starts the chain, and the sound with it." (83; 125 with the clause)

**E-q-14** — `teaser.shut` (and statistical-feedback `teaser.shut`, near-identical). "it is running and nothing reaches you: it is muted, or at zero, on the desktop page." "It is … it is", then the comma-chopped tail (E-c-11). → "it is running but muted or at zero; only the desktop page can change that." (74)

`running.both`, `running.one` ("do two chains share anything?"), `teaser.running.high` ("Where does the style go, and was there ever a phrase in it?") and `teaser.running.low` are good: these are the questions a hand can actually answer, and they stop where they should.

### statistical-feedback

**E-q-15** — `meta.description`. "One switch turns the ledger into dice." The ledger does not become dice; selection stops reading it. The metaphor implies a mechanism the toy does not have. → "One switch stops reading the ledger, and the same weights are dice."

**E-q-16** — `og.description`. "…one switch stops reading them." A switch cannot read. → "Six voices and a silence take turns by the books; one switch makes the same weights dice."

**E-q-17** — `text`, block 1. "**One pulse, seven claimants.** Six voices and a silence, one per turn. Every option keeps a ledger… Playing charges you; the seven bars sum to zero." Three names for the same seven things in three sentences (claimants, voices and a silence, option), then "you" where the voice is meant. "Claimants" is a good word; keep it and let it carry. → "**One pulse, seven claimants.** Six voices and a silence, one per turn. Each keeps a ledger, the bar beside its name, and the turn goes to whoever is furthest behind. Playing charges it; the seven bars sum to zero. Charles Ames' fair share, in one line of arithmetic."

**E-q-18** — `text`, block 3. "A weight at zero leaves the game, ledger frozen, and comes back owing." The voice leaves, not its weight; and the writer's own note says "owing" may be wrong in sign. → "A voice at weight zero leaves the game, its ledger frozen, and comes back where it left off." "Did you write the metre, or did the ledger?" is the best closing question in the draft.

**E-q-19** — `start.notStarted`. "…and one option is chosen on every turn: six voices and a silence." The colon makes seven things the gloss of "one option". → "press ▶ Play, or the Space bar, and one option is chosen on every turn, from six voices and a silence." (102; 134 with the clause)

**E-q-20** — `start.running`. "turn memory off and the same weights become dice. Can you hear it? Watch the debts walk apart, then turn it back on." The sentence stops one beat before the toy's argument (the burst of exactly the voices that were owed), which every other draft keeps; in this style the beat is a question. → "turn memory off and the same weights become dice: can you hear it? Watch the debts walk apart, then turn it back on: who is paid?" (129)

**E-q-21** — `teaser.running`. "turn heterogeneity up: the shares hold, the grain loosens. Does it sound like dice yet?" "Grain" again on the phone (E-c-25); the draft's own `teaser.text` says "the ledger is read less closely" and the rulesheet's phrase for the same thing is "the order loosens", which a thumb understands. → "turn heterogeneity up: the shares hold, the order loosens. Does it sound like dice yet?" (87)

`teaser.text` ("**Can the same shares sound like dice?** … When does it stop sounding like a machine?") is honest about what the phone can test and is the best phone block for this toy in any draft. `share` is good.

### possibles

**E-q-22** — `shows`. "…Does a motif that exists already matter?" See E-q-2. → "You write what can follow what, and a machine walks it aloud. A motif exists: does it matter yet?" (19 words)

**E-q-23** — `meta.description`. "You write a tree of what can follow what and how much it weighs…" "It" is the tree; the weights are on the branches. → "You write a tree of what can follow what and how much each branch weighs; a machine walks it aloud, and nothing you do makes a sound. **E-q-29** — `share`. "Does a motif that exists already matter? One tree, one knob. Write your own on a desktop." See E-q-2. → "A motif exists: does it matter yet? One tree, one knob. Write your own on a desktop." (17 words)

**E-q-30** — `start.notStarted`. "press ▶ Play to start the machine, then write a branch with a strip's write button." "Write … write" in six words, and a possessive chain ("a strip's write button") at 11.5 px. → "press ▶ Play to start the machine, then press write on a strip." (63; 95 with the clause)

## rulesheet

### front

Nothing to object to. The sub, the `what-is` line and all four blurbs are plain, each names a thing and a gesture, and none needs a second reading. This is the front page that works cold.

### motif-memory

**E-r-1** — `meta.description`. "Fidelity blends score and invention at one density." "At one density" is the spec's condition compressed past meaning for a search-result reader. → "Fidelity blends score and invention without changing how much the voice plays."

**E-r-2** — `og.description`. "…one knob blends that memory with invention at the same density." Same compression, now on a share preview where "density" has not been introduced at all. → "…one knob blends that memory with invention without changing how much it plays. Try to hear the crossing."

**E-r-3** — `text`, block 3. "Between, it blends cell by cell at the same density." "Between," standing alone as an adverb is a wince. → "In between it blends cell by cell at the same density." The block's close ("Memory is a state of a voice, not a recorder beside the ensemble, and the crossing can be made inaudible. Try to hear it.") is right, and the writer's reason for "Try to hear it" over "Listen for it" is correct.

**E-r-4** — `teaser.text`. "Turn fidelity down. The music stays as dense. Where it falls starts to move." A clause as the subject of a four-word sentence, on a phone: "Where it falls starts to move" is parsed on the second pass. → "Turn fidelity down: the music stays as dense, but where it falls begins to move. Try to hear the crossing."

**E-r-5** — `teaser.nothing`. "the pulse is running with nothing on it. The desktop page can open the voices." Dropping "density" to keep the phone honest was right; "open the voices" is what is left when the gate metaphor goes and it means nothing on its own. → "nothing is on the pulse: every voice is at zero, and the desktop page can raise them." (85)

**E-r-6** — `teaser.shut`, identical on all four pages. "running, but nothing reaches you. Unmute it on the desktop page." The writer's note already suspects it: when the master is at zero, "unmute it" is wrong and "it" has no referent. → "running, but muted or at zero. Only the desktop page can change that." (69). Four pages, one sentence.

The desktop `text` (Start / Remember / Forget) is the clearest standing text for this toy in any draft; "density is its chance to sound, rate is how often it gets the chance" is a model of the style. Every desktop state sentence is good.

### markov

**E-r-7** — `text`, block 1. "**Start.** Tap any cell: both walkers start on the example. clear empties both; a tap in a rest strip restarts a walker." A tap on a cell adds a step (the block after says so), so the first instruction on the page edits the example the reader has not yet heard. The live copy's "Touch the rack or the trail" starts without writing. And a sentence beginning with a lowercase control name wants bold. → "**Start.** Touch the rack or the trail: both walkers start on the example. **clear** empties both; a tap in a rest row restarts a walker."

## across the drafts

**Habits in more than one draft.**

1. **The phone `shut` sentence was inherited, not written.** All three drafts carry the live copy's comma-chopped "muted, or at zero, on the desktop page" or its cousin "muted or the master is at zero", on every page, and nobody rewrote it. 2. **Nameplates that name what the phone does not show.** The brief's rule is written for `teaser.words`, but `shows` is also the first thing a thumb reads. ## verdict

**Front page** — the rulesheet. Every line is plain, names a thing and a gesture, and reads cold; the claim's sub is the best sub of the three, but two of its four aphorisms (markov, possibles) have to be read twice on the one page where nobody reads twice, and the question draft's sub is a riddle to anyone who has not read the paper.

**Nameplate and meta** — the question draft. Its four `shows` lines each put the claim as a question the thumb can answer, name nothing the phone does not show, and come in at nineteen words; its og lines are the best share previews in the round. (Fix "already" on possibles, and the markov meta's "a chain / two walkers".)

**Standing text** — split by reader. Desktop: the claim draft, whose markov and statistical-feedback blocks are the best prose written in this round ("no two bars are the same bar", "turns taken, less turns owed", "the model's shape, not a shortage of weights") and whose motif-memory block argues the claim the page has never argued. Phone (`teaser.text`): the rulesheet, which says what is on the screen, what to do and what to listen for in that order and in sentences a thumb can hold, with the question draft's sf block ("Can the same shares sound like dice?") the exception that beats it. Share: the claim draft, with the question draft's "The ledger itself wants a desktop" and "Write your own on a desktop" lines close behind.

**State sentences** — the rulesheet. Full stops, one fact a sentence, and nothing to parse twice at 11.5 px; it is also the only draft whose phone `nothing` lines never name a control the phone lacks, even where the result needs rewording (E-r-5). The claim draft is close behind and has the better `running` lines on motif-memory and markov.

**The one note to the author.** The register that works changes with the reader, not with the style you pick: at the desk your aphorism, your "and" chain and your "§3.2" earn their place, and on a phone, cold, the plain declarative sentence beats your best line every time. The drafts that read are the ones that knew which reader they were writing for in that layer (the rulesheet on the phone, the claim at the desk, the question draft on the nameplate). Do not choose a style for the site; choose one per layer, and then let a single hand write the state sentences for all four pages, because the only sentence that is bad in all three drafts is the one none of them wrote.
