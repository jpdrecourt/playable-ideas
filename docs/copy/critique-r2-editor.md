# Critique, round 2 — the fair copy editor

Read against `directions-r1.md`, the three revised drafts and their `## answers`,
and the live pages. For each round-1 objection I checked the copy, not the
answer. Only objections NOT resolved to my satisfaction appear below, as new
numbers continuing round 1 (claim from E-c-31, question from E-q-32, rulesheet
from E-r-27), marked "(re-raises …)"; anything the revision introduced is a
new objection too. Everything else is resolved and gets no entry. State
sentences carry their character count, keyboard clause excluded, inside
145 desktop / 92 phone.

A general finding first, because it explains half of what follows: the
round-1 directions were obeyed, and obeyed *as rules*. Where a writer rewrote
the sentence, the result is better than round 1 almost everywhere. Where a
writer swapped a word to satisfy a rule and left the sentence standing, the
seam shows: "is held" with no agent, "where no weight leads on", "grammar and
all", "a written style", "at zero level", "one strip of odds", "stop being
heard". Those are the objections below. None is large; all are the kind a
thumb reads twice.

## claim

### front

**E-c-31** (re-raises E-c-4, in part) — `blurb motif-memory` and `shows`. "Memory is a state of a voice, and the crossing can be made inaudible." / "Memory is a state of a voice: each remembers what it just played, and the crossing can be made inaudible." Cold, "the crossing" has nothing to cross: round 1 had "crossing into it", which told the reader what side of the line memory is. In `shows`, "each" also has no antecedent (no number of voices has been given). Blurb → "Memory is a state of a voice; crossing into it can be made inaudible." (14 words). `shows` → "Memory is a state of a voice, and the crossing into it can be made inaudible. Five voices, one pulse." (20 words). The lost `<em>` on *fidelity* is the author's call, as the notes say.

### motif-memory

**E-c-32** — `text`, block 1. "Not a recorder beside the ensemble: every voice always keeps its last two bars. Throw its MEM switch and they are its score at the bar line." Two faults the revision introduced. The colon after "ensemble" joins the negation to a fact it does not explain; and "they are its score at the bar line" has gone flat where round 1's "become … at the next bar line" had the event in it (and "next" was the fact). → "**Memory is a state of a voice,** not a recorder beside the ensemble. Every voice keeps its last two bars, unasked. Throw its MEM switch: at the next bar line they become its score. You decide that a passage was worth keeping, never which window to keep: nothing to hit on the beat." (52 words, as before.)

**E-c-33** — `teaser.text` and `teaser.running`. "every voice loops what it just played … Turn it down: the amount of music is held; what moves is where it falls." and "press memory: at the next bar each voice loops. Turn fidelity down and listen for a seam." Three things. "Loops" without an object in `running` ("each voice loops" what?). "Is held" with no agent, on a phone, reads as unfinished (see across-the-drafts). And "seam" is a third name for the one thing this page is about: the front page, nameplate, og and desk all say "crossing", the phone alone says "seam". The writer's reason (the spec's word for the bar line at fidelity 1) is a reason for the spec, not for a thumb that has just read "crossing" in the nameplate above. `teaser.text` → "…Turn it down: fidelity holds the amount of music; what moves is where it falls." `running` → "press memory: each voice repeats itself. Turn fidelity down and listen for the crossing." (88). ("Repeats itself" is plain English for a loop and keeps the second-press guard out of a sentence that could not fit it; if "loops" is wanted, "loops what it played" fits at 86.)

### markov

**E-c-34** — `text`, block 3. "Sixteen weights each, none across them — the independence the paper's §3.2 pays for, made audible. In the instrument a hand plays the matrices; here they walk alone." "Pays for" runs the argument backwards on first reading: the paper does not pay for independence, it settles for it rather than pay the square. And "they walk alone" makes the matrices walk; walkers walk. → "Sixteen weights each, none across them — the independence the paper's §3.2 settles for, made audible. In the instrument a hand plays the matrices; here nobody does."

**E-c-35** — `start.nothing`. "Tap a cell in the row a walker stands on: after clear, a rest row." The tail is a note to the implementer, not a sentence to a visitor: "after clear, a rest row" presumes the reader knows what `clear` does to a walker. The screen already marks the row (the strip is lit). → "the matrices are empty, so neither walker can move. Each stands on a lit row; tap a cell in it." (95)

**E-c-36** — `teaser.notStarted`. "turn structure: at 1 a written style, at 0 dice." A style is not written; weights are. "Grammar" was banned from the phone and "style" was pushed into its slot, which is how the naming rule produced a phrase nobody would say. The draft's own `running.low` has the right words. → "turn structure: at 1 what is written, at 0 dice." (48; 85 with the clause)

**E-c-37** — `teaser.stuck`. "both walkers stand where no weight leads on, so at 1 nothing moves." "Where no weight leads on" is the zero-noun form the cop asked for and it is not English a thumb reads once: "leads on" (onward? deceives?) needs the second pass. → "both walkers stand where nothing follows, so at 1 nothing moves. Turn structure down." (85)

### statistical-feedback

**E-c-38** (re-raises E-c-18) — `shows`. "Six voices and a silence keep books on one pulse." "Keep books on X" means keep records *about* X; the idiom the whole site uses is "share one pulse by keeping books", and that was the clause worth keeping. → "Dice have no memory, and the ear hears the difference. Six voices and a silence take turns by the books." (19 words)

**E-c-39** (re-raises E-c-20) — `og.description`. "…a ledger each, and one switch that stops reading them." The switch still reads. The question draft had the right verb and the directions quoted it. → "…six voices and a silence, a ledger each, and one switch that stops the reading."

**E-c-40** (re-raises E-c-25) — `teaser.running`. "turn heterogeneity up: the same shares, now in clumps and droughts, like dice." Verbless, and shares do not clump; turns do. A participle gives the ear something to wait for. → "turn heterogeneity up: the same shares, arriving in clumps and droughts, like dice." (83)

### possibles

**E-c-41** — `start.running`. "press heavier and, with bias at 0, the machine takes it more often." Press heavier: it weighs more, and at bias 0 the machine takes it more often." (138)

**E-c-42** — `teaser.text` and `teaser.running`. "Left, the written figure sharpens; right, it dissolves, then inverts." / "turn bias and listen: left, the figure sharpens; right, it dissolves, then inverts." ### motif-memory

**E-q-32** — `og.description`, `text` block 2, `start.running`. The noun was added for the thumb and the thumb now reads a typo. → either the 74-character sentence as given ("it is running but muted or at zero; only the desktop page can change that."), or, if the noun is wanted, "it is running but muted, or its level is at zero; only the desktop page can change that." ## across the drafts

**What the directions did, good.** The phone `shut` line is one plain sentence on every page in every draft. The nameplates name nothing the phone lacks and are at or under 20 words everywhere. "Grain" is gone from the phone and nothing worse replaced it ("the order loosens" is the phrase in two drafts and it is right). "Loops" arrived on every motif-memory phone line and it is the best word anyone has found for what memory does to a thumb: short, concrete, and it makes "still a loop?" and "the loop wanders" possible. "Six voices and a silence" is now said wherever it is counted. The memory switch "stops the reading" instead of turning books into dice. The possibles write has one picture. The em-dash count went down and nothing was lost; the "(N)" counts are out of the copy.

**What the directions did, bad.** Four rules were satisfied by token substitution rather than by rewriting, and the token shows:

1. **"Is held."** The directions said *held*, not *never moves*. All three drafts wrote "the amount of music is held" / "how much it plays is held" / "with the amount of music held", a passive with no agent, five times on phones. On a desk, beside "the law holds the amount of music" (question, block 2), it is fine; alone on a phone it reads as an unfinished sentence. The active form costs nothing: "fidelity holds the amount of music; what moves is where it falls." The word *held* survives; the agent returns. (E-c-33, E-r-28; the question draft's `teaser.text` "with the amount of music held" is the least bad of the three and can stand.)
2. **"Where no weight leads on."** The cop's zero-noun form for the phone `stuck` line, taken verbatim by the claim and question drafts and in the question's desktop `running.one` too. It is not a phrase an English reader has met. "Stand where nothing follows" names no control and reads once (E-c-37, E-q-35).
3. **Grammar/style swaps.** The claim draft banned "grammar" from the phone and got "a written style"; the rulesheet was asked to put "grammar" in the teaser body and got "grammar and all"; the question draft was told not to assume the example and got "whatever grammar is written goes to dice". Each is a rule obeyed at the sentence's expense (E-c-36, E-r-31, E-q-36).
4. **"Up to two" / "at zero level" / "with bias at 0".** Correctness clauses dropped into the middle of sentences built without them. The fix every time is to rebuild the sentence around the fact (E-r-28, E-q-33, E-c-41, E-r-37).

**Habits that came back.** The parenthetical condition between commas inside a state line (claim and rulesheet, possibles `running`). The flat verb where round 1 had the event ("they are its score at the bar line"). "Menu" as a bare spec word on two desktops (E-q-41, E-r-35): the live page explained it and the drafts cut the explanation to make budget.

**Where all three went the same way, and it is right.** Every draft now ends its phone `running` on something the ear can check: "still a loop?", "the loop wanders", "listen for the crossing", "hear the walk dissolve into dice", "Does it sound like dice yet?", "Which motifs appear?". That is the single biggest improvement in the round and it came from rule 2.

**Where all three hit the same wall.** The right half of bias. The academic ruled that "the light branches win" is true only past +½, so the claim and question drafts wrote "then inverts" and the rulesheet "then turns contrary"; all three are mechanism words on an ear line. "Dissolves/evens out, then the light branches win" says both halves in the ear's terms and is what I propose in all three (E-c-42, E-q-43, E-r-36). If the academic will not have it, the author should decide which half the phone line is for.

## verdict

**Round-1 objections resolved:** claim 26 of 30 (re-raised E-c-4, 18, 20, 25; 8 new); question 25 of 31 (re-raised E-q-7, 14, 18, 19, 21, 31; 7 new); rulesheet 20 of 26 (re-raised E-r-7, 9, 10, 15, 18, 26 in part; E-r-12 refused with a reason that holds; 5 new). Nothing was marked "taken" that was not in the copy.

**Front page** — the rulesheet, still, by a smaller margin: all six lines are plain and cold-readable, and "A style, but no phrase." is the line of the round. The question draft's front page is now clean too (E-q-1 and E-q-2 taken fully) and would be my second choice; the claim's possibles blurb and motif-memory blurb still need one more pass (E-c-31).

**Nameplate and meta** — the question draft, more clearly than in round 1. Its four `shows` lines each gloss the one dial the phone shows and end on the question; its og lines are the best previews; "the switch stops the reading" was its wording and the others adopted it. The rulesheet's nameplates are close (the possibles one is good once "someone" goes); the claim's are the most aphoristic and the least self-sufficient cold.

**Standing text** — desktop: the claim draft, by its markov and statistical-feedback blocks, with one block (mm block 1, E-c-32) to restore; the rulesheet's motif-memory text (Start / Remember / Invent) is the best standing text for that one toy. Phone (`teaser.text`): the rulesheet, now with the correct bar-count fact and the right order on markov; the question draft's sf block is still the exception that beats it. Share: the claim draft ("The ledgers themselves want a desktop"; "The matrices want a desktop"), with the rulesheet's possibles share equal.

**State sentences** — the rulesheet, by more than in round 1: every phone `running` is a listening cue in plain words, the `shut` line is the shortest and clearest of the three, and its desktop markov lines ("same weights, different walks") teach without a subordinate clause. The claim draft is second; the question draft's `running` lines are the most alive ("still a loop?", "who is paid?") but it has the most token-substitution seams (E-q-32–40).

**The one note to the author.** The round-1 directions worked: the drafts are better in every layer, and the one bad sentence nobody wrote is gone from all four pages. What is left is the residue of rules applied as find-and-replace, and it is the same residue in all three drafts, which means the next round should not be another set of rules. It should be a reader: take the phone layers of whichever draft you pick, give them to someone who has not read the brief, and change every sentence they read twice. The ones they will read twice are listed above.
